      How to read this

      Association, not causation

      Opportunities are linked to an event when the firm attended and the opportunity was created within the window after the event. That tells us what followed events; it does not prove the event caused it. The comparison group (firms that did not attend that event, measured over the same calendar window) and the before/after view are there to test whether attendance is linked to any lift beyond the base rate. Firms self-select into events (and we select them), so even a positive gap would still be association.

      Metric definitions

      • Attended firm: at least one Confirmed contact. Tentative-only firms enter only with the toggle on. "Confirmed" is registration status; the data has no check-in.
      • Associated opportunity: created 0 to W days after an attended event; if several qualify, the most recent event gets credit (last touch). Default W = 90.
      • Associated pipeline: sum of opportunity amounts for associated opportunities, any stage.
      • Committed: opportunity reached Committed on or before the as-of date.
      • Follow-up rate (30d): attending firms with a meeting 1-30 days after the event / attending firms.
      • New-opportunity rate: firms that opened at least one opportunity in the window / firms in the group. Difference-in-differences = (attendee after - before) - (non-attendee after - before).
      • Cost per associated opportunity: event cost / associated opportunities.

      Known limits

      • Three events and 60 firms: every rate here is directional. A one-firm swing moves a tier rate by 5 to 15 points.
      • Events differ in age at the as-of date (Berlin had about 100 days to convert, New York about 195), so commitments favor older events.
      • No opportunity source or campaign field exists, so association relies on timing.
